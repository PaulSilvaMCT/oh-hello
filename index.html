Her history

A lifetime before the paperwork.

Andrea's story did not begin with an insurance company. It began with a family.

Our parents fostered dozens of children over the years. Andrea was one of them. She became their daughter and our sister, and she lived with our parents throughout their lives.

After both of our parents died within a few months of one another, there was no established long-term care plan for Andrea. The responsibility for trying to build one fell to family.

This site will tell that history in full — not because Andrea's past should define her, but because understanding where she came from helps explain why the care she receives today matters so much.

The Care Gap

Hours on paper are not the same as adequate care.

Andrea currently receives a limited number of authorized home-care hours each week.

Outside those hours, many of the same needs remain: meals, household safety, hygiene, judgment, supervision, redirection, companionship and recognition of problems that Andrea may not understand or report on her own.

This site will document what those uncovered hours actually look like in real life.

The public record

Accountability without speculation.

We do not need to guess anyone's motives. We can document what was requested, what was required, what was decided, what was answered, and what happened afterward.

The principle is simple:

What did Andrea need?
What did the system require?
What did we provide?
What decision was made?
What was the consequence for Andrea?

Organizations and individuals discussed on this site will be welcome to provide corrections, explanations or substantive responses. Where appropriate, those responses will be included alongside the record.
