The woman behind the story

Meet
Andrea.

Andrea is 57 years old. She is a sister, a daughter, a member of a very large and unusual family, and a woman whose life cannot be understood by looking at a case number.

She is generally happy. She enjoys familiar people, familiar routines and the comfort of knowing that the world around her makes sense.

Her beginning

She didn't begin as a case. She began as a child.

Our parents opened our family to foster children through the Nassau County Department of Social Services. Over the years, approximately 35 children passed through our home and became part of our family's story.

Andrea was one of those children.

She was born with significant developmental disabilities. Our parents fostered her and eventually adopted her. From that point forward, Andrea was not simply someone receiving care in our home.

She was our sister.

“Oh, hello.”

Andrea lived with our parents throughout their lives. They were the center of the world she understood: the people who knew her routines, her needs, her habits and the small rhythms that helped her feel safe.

Understanding Andrea

Independence is not all or nothing.

Andrea can participate in her own life. She can communicate, make choices, enjoy people, develop relationships and take part in familiar routines. But many ordinary activities require another person to guide, prompt, supervise or assist her.

What familiarity provides

Andrea functions best when the people and routines around her are consistent. Familiar caregivers learn how she communicates, what she needs and when something is wrong.

What support provides

Support is more than completing household tasks. It includes observation, prompting, redirection, reassurance, judgment and recognizing situations Andrea may not recognize herself.

What disruption can mean

Abrupt changes in caregivers or routine can affect Andrea's behavior and her ability to manage ordinary parts of her day.

What dignity means

Andrea's limitations are real, but they are not her identity. Appropriate care should make it possible for her to live her life rather than merely process her through a system.

Family

The people around Andrea matter.

After our father died in October 2020, our mother died only 93 days later. There had never been a long-term transition plan for Andrea's care.

Andrea eventually returned to the New York home where much of our family's story had begun.

Today, family remains an important part of her support. But love from family cannot replace the professional, reliable and sustainable care that Andrea needs.
